The Solar System at true relative speeds

Mercury laps the Sun every 88 days, and Neptune takes 165 years. Run the planets at their true speeds relative to each other and that gap is the whole show: Mercury is a blur while Neptune barely creeps.

88 days against 165 years

Our Solar System live wallpaper covers 1.92 Earth years every minute, about 11.7 days a second. At that pace Mercury goes around in about 7.5 seconds. Neptune would need about 86 minutes for one lap, so in a one-minute loop it hardly moves.

The orbital periods and starting angles are standard published values, set for November 20, 2026.

Our neighborhood, and beyond it

Rows above and below the orbits mark the nearest known planets around other stars and places astronomers have searched for signals. The corners of the backdrop are four James Webb Space Telescope images, dimmed so the orbits stay clear: the Pillars of Creation, the Cosmic Cliffs in Carina, Rho Ophiuchi and Cassiopeia A.
