Kepler-16: the planet with two suns

Kepler-16 b is a planet about as heavy as Saturn that circles two stars at once. The stars, an orange dwarf and a red dwarf, orbit each other every 41 days, and the planet goes around both of them every 229 days.

Reported in 2011, it was the first planet ever seen passing in front of a pair of stars.

Distance

Two suns that eclipse each other

Seen from Earth, the two stars take turns passing in front of each other. When the small star crosses the big one, the light dips about 13%. At the other eclipse it dips about 1.6%.

Neither star sits still. Both circle their shared center of mass, and the planet circles the pair.

Closer in than Venus, still below −70 °C

Kepler-16 b orbits at 0.70 AU, between where Mercury and Venus orbit our Sun. Both of its stars are small and cool, though, so its estimated temperature is only 170 to 200 K, about −103 to −73 °C, with a seasonal swing of about 30 K.

Star B is tiny: when the discovery paper came out in 2011, it was the smallest main-sequence star with such a precisely measured mass and radius.
