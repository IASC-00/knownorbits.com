HD 110067: six planets in a resonant chain

HD 110067 is a star 105 light-years away with six planets, each between Earth and Neptune in size. Their orbit times keep a nearly exact rhythm, 3:2, 3:2, 3:2, 4:3, 4:3, so neighbors line up again and again on a steady beat.

Distance
105 light-years
Star
K0 dwarf, 5,266 K, 0.79 the Sun's radius
Planets
Six sub-Neptunes, b to g
Years
From 9.11 days (b) to 54.77 days (g)
Distances
0.079 to 0.262 AU from the star
Sizes
1.9 to 2.9 times Earth's radius

The rhythm, pair by pair

Divide each planet's year by the year of the planet inside it and you get numbers that sit almost exactly on simple fractions. That's what makes it a resonant chain.

b and c
1.5003, near 3:2
c and d
1.5007, near 3:2
d and e
1.5007, near 3:2
e and f
1.3334, near 4:3
f and g
1.3339, near 4:3

So for every 3 laps of b, c makes 2. For every 4 laps of f, g makes 3.

What's measured, and what isn't

The sizes come from the dips in starlight as each planet crosses the star. The masses are harder. Three are measured: b at 5.7 Earth masses, d at 8.5 and f at 5.0. For c, e and g there are only upper limits, so the wallpaper labels them that way. If they had no air, these planets would sit at about 440 to 800 K, warmest close in.
