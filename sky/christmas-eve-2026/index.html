Christmas Eve 2026 night sky: what's up at 9 PM

Step outside at 9 PM on Christmas Eve, Thursday, December 24, 2026, and the Moon is up in the east, 98% lit, one night past full. Orion stands in the southeast with Sirius below it, Saturn is the steady light about halfway up in the southwest, and Jupiter rises around 9 PM, low in the east-northeast.

The figures are for 40° north, the latitude of Philadelphia, Columbus, Indianapolis, Denver and northern California. At 9 PM local time the sky looks nearly the same in any US time zone.

Moon
East, about 36° up, 98% lit. Full Moon was 8:28 PM Eastern on Dec 23
Saturn

Sirius

Capella
High in the east-northeast, about 67° up

Why one sky works for the whole country

We placed the sky at 40° north, 75° west. That's the middle line of Eastern time, so 9 PM on the clock there is the real 9 PM. Run the same numbers at 9 PM on the middle lines of Central, Mountain and Pacific time and the Moon, Saturn and Jupiter land within about 2° of the same spots. A town far from the middle of its time zone sees this sky up to about 30 minutes earlier or later. North or south of 40°, things sit a little higher or lower.

A bright Moon night

The Moon was full at 8:28 PM Eastern on December 23 and rises around 5:21 PM on Christmas Eve. A nearly full Moon brightens the whole sky, so this is a night for the bright stars and planets, not the faint ones.

The Space Yule Log

For the evening itself, there's our hour-long Space Yule Log. A star burns low like a hearth, and above it hang the winter constellations as they really stood at 9 PM on December 12, 2026, looking east from the US East Coast: Orion rising on its side, Taurus with Aldebaran and the Pleiades, and the twins of Gemini.
