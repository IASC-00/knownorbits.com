TRAPPIST-1: seven Earth-size planets around a small red star

TRAPPIST-1 is a small red star about 40 light-years away, in Aquarius, with seven Earth-size planets. All seven orbit closer to their star than Mercury does to the Sun, so a year there lasts just 1.5 to 19 days.

Distance
About 40 light-years, in Aquarius
Planets
Seven, named b to h
Years
From 1.51 days (b) to 18.77 days (h)

The dips in starlight as each planet crosses its star

A whole system inside Mercury's orbit

The outermost planet, h, circles at 0.062 AU. Mercury sits at 0.387 AU from the Sun, so all seven TRAPPIST-1 orbits would fit inside Mercury's about six times over.

Each one likely keeps one face to the star, the way the Moon keeps one face to Earth.

Planets that keep step like gears

The orbits are linked. While g circles 3 times, f circles about 4 and e about 6. In our live wallpaper each minute shows 36 days, and because the planets move in step, after that span all seven are back close to where they began.

The green band in the wallpaper marks the habitable zone, where a planet with the right air could keep water liquid. It's an estimate, and e, f and g orbit inside it.

How we know they're there

Nobody has photographed these planets. Each one dims the star a little as it crosses in front, and those dips are how they were found. The wallpaper shows them on a light-curve strip. Since nobody has seen their surfaces, we draw them as plain gray globes.
