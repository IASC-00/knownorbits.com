Alpha Centauri: the nearest star system and its real orbit

Alpha Centauri is the nearest star system, about 4.3 light-years away. Its two bright stars, A and B, circle each other every 79.8 years, swinging between 11 and 35 AU apart, and their next closest pass is in 2035.

The third star, Proxima Centauri, is a red dwarf and the closest star to us. It takes about 550,000 years to go once around A and B.

Two stars, one balance point

Neither star orbits the other. Both circle their center of mass, an empty balance point between them. A is about 19% heavier, so it stays closer to that point and swings the smaller loop. Their last closest pass was in 1875, and the next comes around 2035.

An AU is the distance from Earth to the Sun, so even at their closest the two stars are 11 times farther apart than we are from ours.

Proxima and its planets

Proxima b has at least 1.06 Earth masses and goes around its star every 11.2 days, about eight times closer than Mercury is to the Sun. It sits in Proxima's habitable zone, but Proxima is a flare star, and the planet gets blasted. Whether it has air or water isn't known yet.

Proxima d is smaller, at least 0.26 Earth masses, with a 5.1-day year. Proxima c was reported in 2020 and is still disputed, so we draw it on a dashed ring and label it unconfirmed. S1, a possible Saturn-mass planet around A, was seen once by JWST in 2024. It's a candidate, not a confirmed planet.
