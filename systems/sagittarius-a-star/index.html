Sagittarius A*: the stars that orbit our galaxy's black hole

Sagittarius A* is the black hole at the center of the Milky Way, about 27,000 light-years away, with about 4.3 million Suns' worth of mass. You can't see it, but you can watch the S-stars that whip around it on measured orbits. The brightest, S2, laps it every 16 years.

S2's close pass

On May 19, 2018, the star S2 passed 120 AU from Sagittarius A*. That's about four times Neptune's distance from the Sun. It was moving at about 7,650 km/s, a quick 2.5% of the speed of light.

S2's orbit also slowly turns, about 12 arcminutes a lap, an effect detected in 2020. That's too small to see in our drawing, so it isn't drawn.

S29 went faster

In 2021 the star S29 swung by at roughly 100 AU and about 8,740 km/s, even faster than S2. Its own orbit is long, about 91 years.

A very small patch of sky

The whole view in our video is about 1.3 arcseconds tall. At that distance one arcsecond is about 8,277 AU. The black hole itself isn't drawn, since there's nothing to see. A small + marks the spot. Blue-white dots are early-type, hotter stars and orange ones are late-type, cooler stars.
