Credits

Known Orbits uses other people's measurements and images. Each one is listed here with its license and what was changed.

Data

Facts and measurements aren't owned by anyone. Here's where each one came from.

Planets around other stars

  • NASA Exoplanet Archive, queried November 20, 2026. This research has made use of the NASA Exoplanet Archive, which is operated by the California Institute of Technology, under contract with the National Aeronautics and Space Administration under the Exoplanet Exploration Program.
  • TRAPPIST-1 (shirt and wallpaper): Agol et al. 2021 (sizes, masses, orbits); Ducrot et al. 2020 (transit times, periods); Gillon et al. 2017; Vida et al. 2017 (flares); Kopparapu et al. 2014 (habitable zone, an estimate, labeled "possible"); JWST results from Greene et al. 2023, Zieba et al. 2023, Piaulet-Ghorayeb et al. 2025, Espinoza et al. 2025 and Lim et al. 2026.
  • Alpha Centauri (shirt and wallpaper): Akeson et al. 2021 (the orbit of A and B); Suárez Mascareño et al. 2025 (Proxima b and d); Kervella et al. 2016 (distance) and 2017 (Proxima's distance from A and B); Ribas et al. 2016; Faria et al. 2022; Anugu et al. 2026 (Proxima's size); Damasso et al. 2020 and Benedict & McArthur 2020 (Proxima c, disputed, shown as unconfirmed); Beichman et al. 2025 (S1, a candidate, wallpaper only).
  • Kepler-90 (shirt and wallpaper): Cabrera et al. 2014; Shallue & Vanderburg 2018; Weiss et al. 2024; Fulton & Petigura 2018; Shaw et al. 2025; Thompson et al. 2018 (Kepler DR25).
  • Kepler-16 (shirt and wallpaper): Doyle et al. 2011, Science 333, 1602.
  • 55 Cancri (shirt): Bourrier et al. 2018 (planet e); Moutou et al. 2026 (planets b, c, f, d); Eggenberger et al. 2004 and Mugrauer et al. 2006 (the companion star B).
  • HD 110067 (wallpaper): Luque et al. 2023, Nature 623, 932.
  • TOI-178 (wallpaper): Leleu et al. 2021, A&A 649, A26, and Leleu et al. 2024, A&A 688, A211.

Our Solar System, the Moon and the sky

  • NASA JPL, Approximate Positions of the Planets (Keplerian elements, 1800–2050). Planet positions for January 1, 2027 on the Planets 2027 and Real Systems: Solar System shirts.
  • NASA JPL planetary ephemeris DE440 (Park et al. 2021, AJ 161, 105), read with the open-source Skyfield library. Sun, Moon and planet positions for the calendar, star maps, eclipse prints, topic posters and topic wallpapers, and for the Moon line on this site's home page.
  • U.S. Naval Observatory, Astronomical Applications API, Moon phases 2027. The Moon 2027 shirt, and a check on every Moon phase time used elsewhere.
  • Star fields behind the wallpapers: UCAC4, the fourth U.S. Naval Observatory CCD Astrograph Catalog (Zacharias et al. 2013, AJ 145, 44), retrieved through the VizieR service (CDS, Strasbourg), catalog I/322A. Star colors from the 2MASS J magnitudes carried in UCAC4. This product makes use of data products from the Two Micron All Sky Survey, which is a joint project of the University of Massachusetts and the Infrared Processing and Analysis Center/California Institute of Technology, funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and the National Science Foundation.
  • Yale Bright Star Catalogue, 5th revised edition (Hoffleit & Warren 1991), NASA Astronomical Data Center, CDS catalog V/50. The stars on the calendar and star maps. Star names from the IAU Working Group on Star Names; the 88 constellation names are the IAU's.
  • Eclipse Predictions by Fred Espenak, NASA's GSFC, from the NASA/GSFC Eclipse Web Site, including NASA's Five Millennium Catalog of Solar Eclipses. Eclipse dates, paths and durations, used for and checked against the eclipse prints and posters.
  • International Meteor Organization, 2027 Meteor Shower Calendar (ed. J. Rendtel). Meteor shower peaks and rates on the calendar and the meteor shower chart, in our own words.
  • National Astronomical Observatory of Japan, Celestial Phenomena 2027, and NASA JPL Horizons. Used only to check our numbers; nothing reproduced.
  • Time zones: the IANA time zone database (public domain).
  • Light travel times on the You are looking at the past shirt are standard values: the Moon's mean distance of 384,400 km, 499.0 light-seconds to the Sun, Alpha Centauri 4.37 light-years, Sirius 8.6, the Pleiades about 444, Andromeda 2.5 million.

Earth and science

  • NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, monthly mean carbon dioxide at Mauna Loa, Hawaii. March 1958 to April 1974 are Scripps Institution of Oceanography data (C. D. Keeling). October 2026 is preliminary. The 315.7 to 426.7 shirt.
  • The Lorenz equations (E. N. Lorenz, 1963) with their classic parameters, and the golden angle, 137.5°: mathematics, drawn by Known Orbits.

Topic posters and wallpapers (being added)

  • NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, CGI Moon Kit: Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter camera color mosaic and LOLA laser altimeter relief, for the Moon posters and wallpaper. Landing sites measured from LRO images (NASA NSSDCA, LROC).
  • Mars Global Surveyor laser altimeter (MOLA), NASA, for Mars heights.
  • NASA NSSDCA planetary fact sheets and JPL planetary satellite elements, for planet diameters, Moon distances, and Mars and its moons Phobos and Deimos.
  • JPL Small-Body Database, for the orbits of 1I, 2I and 3I, the three known interstellar visitors.
  • GRAVITY Collaboration 2020, A&A 636, L5, for the orbit of the star S2 around the black hole at the center of our galaxy, measured with ESO's Very Large Telescope.
  • Natural Earth land outlines (public domain), for the eclipse map and the 2027 eclipse wallpaper.
